How to read the table

  • Fields and trees show growth: one full field or one tree. Workers plant and pick; more of them do not make a plant grow faster.
  • Gatherers and kitchens show the most one building can make in its work shift if its inputs are already there and nobody has to walk. Real output is lower, often by half or more: walking and carrying are most of a villager’s day (see Roads and walking).
  • A food unit is how filling one item is, as the game stores it on the item: a Bread is 2, a Cheese 3, a Cookie or a Fruit 1 (the Food page lists every item). Each unit adds 60 to a villager’s Food meter, so one Bread fills a villager as much as two Cookies. That is why the table counts food units, not items.
  • Whole-chain work adds up every worker-second from the raw source to the food: sowing and harvesting, milling, chopping firewood, picking, cooking. Lower is cheaper. Farmland is the field or orchard squares it takes to feed one villager. Walking is in neither, so treat both as best cases for comparing, not as plans.
  • Net food subtracts the food a kitchen cooks with. Villagers fed divides that by the 0.67 food units one villager burns a day (see How eating works).

  • Cookies cost more than Bread per food unit, not per item. One Flour makes 8 loaves of Bread or 8 Cookies. But a Bread is worth 2 food units and a Cookie 1, so ten food units of Cookies take 1.3 Wheat, 2.5 Eggs and 1.3 Sticks, against 0.63 Wheat and 0.5 Sticks for Bread. What the Eggs and the extra Flour buy is Fun: each Cookie also gives 4 Fun.
  • Wheat is the cheapest ground for food, once it is baked. A Wheat field grows 1 Wheat in 12 days, and one Wheat bakes into 16 food units of Bread: about 1.3 food units a day per field, against 0.33 for a Carrot field eaten raw. The catch is volume: keeping a Bakery fully busy takes dozens of fields (see the table), so in practice the fields, not the Bakery, set how much Bread you get.

Chicken Coop output is a player report (5 Eggs a coop a day), not decoded. Field counts use one harvest per planting and the stored growth days, with no time lost to replanting.

What stands out

  • Counting the whole chain, the cheapest food per worker is Cheese (Cheesery) 33 s, Potato eaten raw 38 s, Tomato eaten raw 38 s and Barley Porridge (Porridge Cookhouse) 45 s of work per 10 food units, from raw source to plate. The costliest are Manchamanteles (Desert Cookhouse) 130 s and Stroganoff (Stroganoff Cookhouse) 125 s: long recipes, and food cooked from other food. Bread (51 s) is also light on ground: about 0.5 squares of Wheat per villager fed, against 2 or more for vegetables or fruit eaten raw.
  • A kitchen out-feeds any field by far, as long as its inputs keep coming. A fully staffed Bakery can turn out about 87.1 food units a day; the best field, Carrot, grows 0.33. The fields still matter: they are what the kitchens cook.
  • Cooking food into food gains less than it looks. The Desert Cookhouse turns 1 Fruit and 1 Veggie (2 food units) into 3 Manchamanteles (3 units): a gain of 1 per pot. An Open Kitchen cooking Veggies turns 2 units into 3; fed Meat or Eggs, which villagers cannot eat raw, every unit it makes is new food.
  • Past a point, more workers add little. A step with a fixed number of spots (one stove, a rack of timed spots, the barley plots) caps these buildings: Cheesery (57.5 food units a day with 1, 81 with 2), Fondue Bar (73.6 food units a day with 2, 81.9 with 3), Open Kitchen (2 workers make as much as 3), Porridge Cookhouse (1 worker makes as much as 2), Stroganoff Cookhouse (17.9 food units a day with 1, 20.7 with 2). The “Limited by” column says which step.
  • Gatherers are capped by the trees, not their workers. A full Foragers’ Hut could pick about 87.1 Fruit a day, but a fruit tree grows only about 0.3 to 0.61. Count the trees in reach to know what it will really bring in.
  • The Porridge Cookhouse grows its own barley, so it needs only Sticks for its fire: about 28.8 food units a day from no food at all. Its three barley plots, not its workers, set the pace.

Where the numbers come from

Fields use the stored harvest and growth days, trees the stored fruit intervals, as on the Food page. For buildings, each step of the recipe has a stored animation length and a count of repeats. Adding up the worker steps gives worker-seconds per batch; a worker has 180 seconds of shift a day. A step done at a single spot also caps the building, however many workers it has. The figure shown is whichever limit comes first. Gatherers use the extraction time of a typical source (6.2 s for a fruit drop). Cookies and Gingerbread Men also give Fun, which this table does not count.

Whole-chain work adds, for each input, the work to bring one unit to the kitchen: Wheat 7.6 s (sown and harvested in a Wheat field); Flour 11.2 s (1 Wheat, ground (no worker) and bagged at the Windmill); Veggie 3.8 s (sown and harvested in a Potato field); Fruit 6.2 s (picked from a fruit tree); Stick 4.5 s (a Log felled from a timber tree and split at the Firewood Yard (1 Log -> 4 Sticks)); Egg 5.3 s (collected at the Chicken Coop; laying and cleaning not counted); Meat 19 s (one cast at the Fishing Dock, taken as 1 Meat (stored catch 0-1 or 0-2 a cast)); Mushroom 13.8 s (picked from a wild mushroom); Cheese 9.8 s (made at the Cheesery (milking included), with its fire Sticks). A field’s work is its sowing spot plus each plant’s harvest, divided by its yield. Farmland uses the same fields and the best fruit tree per square.
