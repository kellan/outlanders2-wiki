MECHANICS

Needs

What each villager need is, how fast it falls, and everything that restores it: items with their stored units, gathering places, gear and sleep. Stored settings read as named; the in-game test protocols are in the repository.

The eight needs at a glance

Every need is a meter. It falls by a set amount each day and is topped up by eating or drinking something, visiting a place, wearing gear, or sleeping. Three of them kill when they run out. The numbers are the stored settings; nobody has timed them in play.

Meter points = units × what one unit restores for that need. A 2-unit Bread is 120 points on a Food meter that falls 40 a day; a 1.2-unit plaza visit is 30 points on a Community meter that falls 20 a day.

How the meters work

Each need is a meter. It starts at the first value, falls by the daily amount, and every unit on a food, drink or other item restores the “one unit” amount. Below the low warning villagers complain; above the content line the need counts as met; the meter cannot rise past its highest value. Three needs can kill: Food (hunger) at −100, Heat (cold) and Hydration (thirst) at −50. These are the stored settings; nobody has timed them in play yet.

If the numbers behave as named, a villager uses about 0.67 food units a day (40 lost, 60 restored per unit), and 0.8 drink units and 0.8 heat units where those needs are switched on. A Bread carries 2 food units, so that is about 0.33 loaves a day per villager.

How eating works

Villagers do not eat on a schedule. The Food meter starts a new villager at 130 and tops out at 150; it falls 40 a day. From full, a villager stays Full for 1 day, counts as Satisfied until day 1.5, is merely Wanting until day 3.25, then Low, and starves only after about 6.25 days with nothing at all. Hunger is a slow slide, so a short gap in bread does no harm.

When they choose to eat

Eating competes with work, sleep and play. The game scales the urge to eat by how hungry the villager is; a Full villager never eats, a Satisfied one eats only when little else calls, a hungry one drops almost everything.

Eating at a kitchen or other eating place starts a touch higher (0.99), so a villager with both options slightly prefers company. No stored field expresses a liking for particular dishes; if villagers favour one food over another beyond its units, that lives in code.

What one meal is worth

Each food unit restores 60 on the meter, but the meter cannot pass 150. A rich food eaten by a barely hungry villager wastes part of its value: a 2-unit Bread eaten at Satisfied (90) can only add 60 of its 120. The “worth it below” column is the meter value under which the whole item counts.

Seen in play

  • Over the first five days of a fresh town, nine villagers with no kitchen ate 36 food units, 4 each: 0.8 units per villager per day. One adult was seen eating four times: day 1 early night, day 2 night, day 4 dawn and day 5 morning. tested in play, 2026-10-26

    Evergreen sandbox, Maira, 9 villagers, 18 Fruit and 36 Veggies to start, no production, no houses. Matches the decoded Food meter: it starts at 130, falls 40 a day and one unit is worth 60, so five days need about 3.3 to 4 units each once the starting fill is used. Meals did not come at a fixed clock position. A finite starting window, not a long-run ration; food never ran out.

Villager life and happiness

How long a villager lasts when a need runs out, ages and births are on Villagers and life. How met needs add up to happiness, and the bonus each gives, is on Happiness. Heat, Cooling and Water have their own page: Heat, cooling and water.

Housing

Housing is a need switch on levels but has no resource; it is met by houses. The Houses page compares every house: villager spots, crib and doghouse, cost per villager and where each can be built, and explains why spare houses keep births going.
