MECHANICS

Heat, cooling and water

The three needs that depend on the map: how long villagers last without them, what keeps them warm, and the weather that changes the rates.

All mechanicsNeedsHow eating worksHappinessVillagers and lifeHeat, cooling and waterDays and timeGearHousesGathering placesSandbox

Which levels

These needs are off unless a level switches them on: Heat on 39 of 130 levels, Cooling on 30 and Water on 4. A level page’s Needs row says which are on.

Winter, in the game’s words: Extreme low temperatures in the Winterlands make survival tough. Being cold for too long will result in death. Satisfying this need activates the Boost, making it easier to satisfy every other need.

Tropical, in the game’s words: High temperatures in the Tropiclands make daytime work tough. Lack of Cooling will reduce a villager's movement speed. Satisfying this need activates the Boost, making it easier to satisfy every other need.

Desert, in the game’s words: The conditions of the Drylands are a challenge for outlanders. Being dehydrated for too long will result in death. Satisfying this need activates the Boost, making it easier to satisfy every other need.

How long villagers last

Cold and thirst kill. Cooling does not; the game says a lack of it slows villagers down, and it counts towards happiness with the same weight. Meeting any of the three gives the Boost: every other meter falls 20% more slowly.

Worked from the stored meter: (meter − death value) ÷ fall per day, with nothing restoring it. A met survival need makes the others fall 20% slower, which stretches these times. Real time is at normal speed, five minutes a game day. Nobody has timed a death in play yet.

Keeping warm

One Heat unit is 25 points on a meter that falls 20 a day. A Bonfire needs a keeper only to light it: the keeper feeds in fuel and can leave, and the fire burns on until the fuel runs out.

Weather and other events

Tropical maps run a weather event every 2 to 6 days (10 minutes to 30 minutes), announced two days ahead.
