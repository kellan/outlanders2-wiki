How the spots work

A house's villager spots are its sleeping places; the game counts a house as full when they are taken. Each house also has one crib (a one-sleeper that accepts only babies): a newborn sleeps there, not in a villager spot, until it becomes a kid at age 3. Each also has a one-sleeper doghouse that accepts no villagers; the files hold Dog Adult and Dog Baby prefabs with a hide-in-kennel activity, but when a dog appears in play is not recorded. Whether kids and elders count against the same spots as adults, and whether a family can split across houses, has not been tested.

Why spare houses matter

Births are checked against the stored reproduction values: a base chance of 14% per check for every 2 adults, raised by happiness (×0.4 of it) and lowered by 40% per child already per adult. With no free housing the chance is multiplied by 0.025, so a settlement with full houses all but stops having babies. Players find that building as many houses as possible is the surest way to get babies, and the data fits that: every house has exactly one crib, a baby occupies it until age 3, so the number of houses caps how many babies can be around at once, and "free housing" is most likely counted in free cribs. Population is capped at 200. How often the check runs is not stored. This is a reading of the stored values, not a tested rule.
