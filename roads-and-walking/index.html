MECHANICS

Roads and walking

How fast villagers walk, how much roads speed them up, and the gear, needs and boosts that change it. These are stored speeds and multipliers; nobody has timed them in play yet.

When to build a road

  1. A Road cuts walking time by about 30%. Villagers walk ×1.4 on it, so every 10 seconds of walking becomes about 7. It works the same loaded or empty. Picking things up and putting them down take as long as before.
  2. It is like adding haulers. Seven villagers who spend their day walking on Roads get through the walking of about 10.
  3. Pave long, busy routes first, the ones walked many times a day: storage to the busiest workplaces, homes to food and water, and the builders’ route to new sites. A short hop beside a warehouse hardly changes.
  4. Keep it unbroken. Speed is set by the tile under each step, so a gap in the road is walked at bare-ground speed.
  5. Boots and Roads overlap. Boots give ×1.4 on bare ground but only ×1.2 on a Road. For an adult in Boots, a Road adds about 20% rather than 40%. Roads pay most before your villagers have Boots, and for children and elders, who never wear them.

What a road costs, and when it pays back

Setting up, once: Roads are laid by the Roadworks Office, which costs 6 Logs. That is one and a half Pine Trees: about 60 seconds of chopping (6 chops of 6.67 seconds per tree), then carrying the Logs to the Lumberjacks’ Base and on to the site, then about 26 seconds of building (4 spots × 10 rounds). With the trees and the site a 6 to 12 second walk from the stores, that comes to 5.5–8.2 minutes of villager work, 1.8–2.7 shifts. Most of it is carrying.

Each Road tile costs no materials, only work: one 5-second job for a road worker plus the step to the next tile, about 6 seconds. The office’s workers do nothing else while they lay road; take them off once the road is done.

Paying back: a Road gives back 30% of the time villagers spend walking on it.

  • A tile pays for its own work once villagers have spent about 20 seconds in total walking over it. On any route walked a few times a day, that is within the first day.
  • The office pays back once roads have saved its 6–8 minutes. For example, three haulers who each spend half their shift walking on the new roads save about 77 seconds a day, so the office pays for itself in about 4–6 days. Every day after that is profit.
  • So it is rarely worth it for a level with only a few days left, or for a village with one or two haulers on short trips. It nearly always is for a long level with busy routes.

Walking speed

Every villager has a stored walking speed, with a small random spread of ±0.1 so that a crowd does not move in step. Carrying something slows anyone to ×0.8.

Roads and other surfaces

Some tiles speed up anyone walking on them. A plain Road adds 40%. Plazas, parks and warehouse floors add a little. The rare Stone and Brick Roads more than double walking speed.

Other things that change speed

  • Boots: ×1.4 on bare ground and ×1.2 on any faster surface, adults only.
  • Slopes: walking downhill is faster and uphill slower, in proportion to the steepness, up to a cap.
  • Fun met (village-wide): walking ×1.2.
  • Food met (village-wide): walking while carrying ×1.25.
  • Cooling running low (village-wide): walking ×0.75.
  • Carrying: ×0.8 while holding something.
  • Speed Boost (Tournament Mode): ×1.5 walking and ×1.5 carrying.

How they combine

The game multiplies them together for every step a villager takes:

speed = age speed × slope × (0.8 × carry bonuses, if carrying) × walking bonuses × surface × Boots

  • The surface is the tile being stepped onto.
  • Boots give their smaller ×1.2 on any tile faster than bare ground, including plazas and warehouse floors, and ×1.4 everywhere else. An adult in Boots on a Stone Road walks ×2.58.
  • The Fun, Food and Cooling effects are village-wide: they come from the village’s needs, not each villager’s, and apply to everyone.

So with Food and Fun both met, a hauler carrying a load along a Stone Road moves ×2.58 of normal speed, against ×0.8 on bare ground with neither.

Read from the game code (version 31). The last step, where the game applies this factor to the walker, runs in compiled code that has not been read, and the slope cap is stored in the level scene, which the files here do not include.
