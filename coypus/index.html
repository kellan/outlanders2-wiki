MECHANICS

Coypus

The Wetlands’ trappable animal. Coypus nest in burrows beside Cypress Trees, and the Swamp Trappers' Hut catches them for Meat and Animal Hides.

Burrows

Only Cypress Trees grow coypu burrows, so coypus live wherever Cypress Trees grow: wild in the Wetlands and on Oona’s maps. A burrow sits right beside its tree, on sand or soil.

  • Each Cypress Tree has at most one burrow at a time.
  • Every 6–10 days the tree grows a new burrow, if its last one has gone. If the old burrow is still there, the tree waits another 6–10 days, so trap burrows quickly.
  • On a new map about half the Cypress Trees already have a burrow, and the rest get one within the first 2 days.
  • Each burrow is trapped once, then disappears.

Trapping

  1. During their shift, the trappers from a Swamp Trappers' Hut carry an empty trap to a burrow and set it.
  2. The trap catches a coypu that night, late on the night face of the dial. Nobody needs to be there.
  3. On their next shift the trappers bring the full trap back, and the burrow disappears.
  4. At the hut, each full trap gives 2 Meat and 1 Animal Hide, and the trap is used again.

A hut has up to 3 trappers and works the usual shift (work shifts).

How far a hut reaches

A hut traps only the burrows inside its area: a rounded square 14 squares across, centred on the hut. That reaches 6 squares out from each side of the hut, a little less towards the corners. Burrows outside it are never trapped, however idle the trappers are, so build the hut among Cypress Trees.

How many trees you need

Each Cypress Tree inside a hut’s area gives at most one coypu about every 8 days: 2 Meat and 1 Animal Hide.

Looking after the trees

  • A Cypress Tree can’t be felled while its burrow is being trapped. At any other time a Lumberjacks' Base or Foresters' Hub that reaches it can fell it, and every tree felled is one burrow fewer. Keep their areas away from the trees your trappers use.

The real animal

The coypu, also called the nutria, is a large semi-aquatic rodent from South America. An adult weighs about 4–9 kg, with a body 40–60 cm long and a long, round, almost hairless tail.

  • Not a capybara. Both are South American rodents that live by the water, but the capybara is the largest rodent alive: 35–66 kg, over a metre long, with only a stub of a tail. A coypu is about the size of a large house cat, with a long rat-like tail. It is more often mistaken for a muskrat or a small beaver.
  • Burrows by the water. Coypus dig their burrows in the banks of marshes and rivers, swim on webbed hind feet, and eat marsh plants, with willow bark in winter. They are most active at dusk and around midnight.
  • Orange teeth. Their big front teeth are bright orange, stained by iron in the enamel, and keep growing all their lives.
  • Fur and meat. Coypus have been farmed and trapped for their fur since the late 1800s. Escaped and released animals spread through North America, Europe and Asia, where they strip wetlands of plants and are treated as a pest. Louisiana pays trappers to catch them and has encouraged people to eat them: the meat is lean and is compared to rabbit.
  • The name. “Nutria” is Spanish for otter, and the game’s files call the coypu spawner on Cypress Trees the “Otter Spawner”.
